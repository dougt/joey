Kangaroo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A kangaroo is a marsupial from the family Macropodidae (macropods, meaning "large foot"). In common use the term is used to describe the largest species from this family, the Red Kangaroo, the Antilopine Kangaroo, and the Eastern and Western Grey Kangaroo. Kangaroos are endemic to the continent of Australia.

A young kangaroo is called a joey. Joeys are born very small and continue developing in the mother's pouch for several months before venturing out.

Kangaroos have large, powerful hind legs, large feet adapted for leaping, a long muscular tail for balance, and a small head.
